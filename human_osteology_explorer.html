Skeletal Anatomy Divisions

Axial vs. Appendicular Skeleton

The human skeleton is divided into two primary structural frameworks designed for central core protection and versatile limb mobility.

80 Bones

Axial Skeleton

Forms the central vertical axis of the body, protecting vital organs of the brain, spinal cord, heart, and lungs. It acts as the primary support pillar and attachment site for postural muscles.

1. Skull (Cranium & Facial Bones - 28 bones)

Protects the brain and special sense organs; houses auditory ossicles (malleus, incus, stapes).

2. Vertebral Column (26 bones)

Comprising cervical (7), thoracic (12), lumbar (5), sacrum (1 fused), and coccyx (1 fused) vertebrae.

3. Thoracic Cage (Sternum & Ribs - 25 bones)

Encloses thoracic viscera; provides attachment for pectoral girdle and upper respiratory muscles.

126 Bones

Appendicular Skeleton

Comprises the bones of upper and lower limbs, plus the pectoral and pelvic girdles that attach limbs to the axial skeleton. Engineered for locomotion, manipulation, and weight-bearing.

1. Pectoral Girdle & Upper Limbs (64 bones)

Clavicles, scapulae, humerus, radius, ulna, carpals, metacarpals, and phalanges.

2. Pelvic Girdle (Hip Bones / Coxal Bones - 2 bones)

Ilischium, ischium, and pubis fused together; connects lower limbs securely to the axial skeleton.

3. Lower Limbs (60 bones)

Femur, patella, tibia, fibula, tarsals, metatarsals, and phalanges engineered for high impact and gait.

Microscopic Anatomy

Bone Histology & Cellular Physiology

Understand bone as a living, dynamic tissue composed of specialized cells, mineralized extracellular matrix, and complex vascular channels.

Compact vs. Spongy Bone

Compact Bone (Cortical): Dense, smooth, and solid outer layer providing mechanical strength and protection.

Spongy Bone (Cancellous/Trabecular): Honeycomb network of trabeculae filled with red bone marrow, reducing skeletal weight while resisting multi-directional stress.

Osteons (Haversian Systems)

The structural unit of compact bone. Consists of concentric rings of calcified matrix (lamellae) surrounding a central canal (Haversian canal) containing blood vessels and nerves. Nutrients diffuse through tiny fluid-filled channels called canaliculi connecting lacunae.

Primary Bone Cells

• Osteoblasts: Bone-forming cells that synthesize osteoid matrix.
• Osteocytes: Mature bone cells residing in lacunae maintaining daily matrix health.
• Osteoclasts: Multinucleated cells derived from monocytes responsible for bone resorption and mineral homeostasis.
